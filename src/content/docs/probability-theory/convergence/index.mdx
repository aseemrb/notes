In this section, we study the different ways a sequence of random variables can converge to a limit. This is central to probability theory, as it allows us to approximate complex random systems with simpler ones (like the General Limit Theorems).

## Key topics

1.  **[Modes of Convergence](modes)**: Almost sure, in probability, in $L^p$, and in distribution.
2.  **[Convergence Hierarchy](hierarchy)**: How the different modes of convergence relate to each other.
3.  **[Weak Convergence](weak-convergence)**: A deep dive into convergence in distribution, the weakest but arguably most useful mode.
4.  **[Skorohod's Theorem](skorohod)**: Relates weak convergence to almost sure convergence via a change of probability space.
5.  **[Portmanteau Theorem](portmanteau)**: Equivalent definitions of weak convergence involving open/closed sets and bounded continuous functions.
6.  **[Helly's Selection Theorem](helly)**: A compactness result for sequences of distribution functions.
7.  **[Tightness Condition](tightness)**: Crucial condition for the existence of convergent subsequences.
8.  **[Continuity Theorem](continuity)**: The link between weak convergence of measures and pointwise convergence of characteristic functions (Lévy's Continuity Theorem).
