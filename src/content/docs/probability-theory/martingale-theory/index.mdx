Martingale theory studies stochastic processes whose increments are unpredictable given the past: the mathematical model of a **fair game**. Built on [conditional expectation](../conditional-expectation/) and filtrations, it supplies tools, stopping times and the optional sampling theorem, that turn intuitive statements about gambling and random walks into theorems.

## Key topics

1.  **[Martingales](martingales)**: Filtrations and adapted **stochastic processes**, then the definition of a **martingale** (sub- and supermartingales) as a process whose conditional increment given the past is zero.
2.  **[Properties](properties)**: The elementary consequences of the martingale property, and the **discrete stochastic integral** (martingale transform) describing the gains of a predictable betting strategy.
3.  **[Stopping Times](stopping-times)**: Random times that depend only on the past, and the **stopped process** obtained by freezing a martingale at a stopping time.
4.  **[Optional Sampling](optional-sampling)**: The **optional sampling theorem** extending the fair-game identity from fixed times to stopping times, under uniform integrability or a bounded stopping time.
5.  **[Random Walk](random-walk)**: Three results on the simple random walk: **gambler's ruin** via optional sampling, **Pólya's recurrence** across dimensions, and the scaling limit to **Brownian motion**, with interactive animations.
